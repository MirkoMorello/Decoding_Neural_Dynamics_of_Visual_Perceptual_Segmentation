\chapter{Discussion}
\label{c:conc}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------

\epigraph{\enquote{Computer Science is no more about computers than astronomy is about telescopes.}}{\emph{E. W. Dijkstra}}


This thesis set out to investigate a central hypothesis: that the accuracy of computational models of human gaze can be significantly improved by moving beyond a purely feature-based understanding of scenes to one that explicitly incorporates their structural composition. Through an iterative process of model development and rigorous empirical evaluation, we have presented a body of work that provides strong evidence in support of this claim. This chapter contextualizes our principal findings within the broader literature, discusses their implications, acknowledges the limitations of our approach, and outlines promising avenues for future research.

\section{Summary of Contributions and Principal Findings}
\label{sec:discussion_summary}

The core contribution of this thesis is the development and validation of \textbf{DinoGaze-SPADE}, a novel, state-of-the-art probabilistic architecture for scanpath prediction. Our research narrative unfolded through a series of controlled experiments, each yielding a key finding:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A Superior Foundational Representation:} We first demonstrated that simply replacing the CNN backbone of the previous state-of-the-art model, `DeepGaze III`, with a DINOv2 Vision Transformer (`DinoGaze`) results in a substantial performance increase. This establishes that the global, context-aware features learned through self-supervision provide a more powerful and structurally-informed foundation for gaze prediction than the local, hierarchical features of classification-trained CNNs.

    \item \textbf{A Novel Mechanism for Information Injection:} We introduced \textbf{"semantic painting,"} a new technique that solves the critical "semantic incoherence" problem, allowing for the effective injection of unsupervised segmentation masks into a network's processing stream via Spatially-Adaptive Normalization (SPADE).

    \item \textbf{Validation of Explicit Segmentation Guidance:} We provided conclusive empirical evidence that this explicit structural guidance improves predictive performance. Our hybrid model (`DG-SPADE v3`) showed that a superior semantic "vocabulary" from DINOv2 could boost a weaker DenseNet backbone. Our final `DinoGaze-SPADE` model demonstrated that these architectural innovations generalize robustly to the challenging, sequential task of scanpath prediction, setting a new state of the art on the MIT1003 benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to the State of the Art and Broader Implications}
\label{sec:discussion_literature}

Our work both builds upon and extends several key threads in the literature on visual attention modeling.

\paragraph{An Extension of the DeepGaze Paradigm.} Our research can be seen as a direct extension of the probabilistic scanpath modeling paradigm established by `DeepGaze III` \cite{kummerer2022deepgaze}. While we retain its core probabilistic framework and modular structure (separating spatial and scanpath processing), our work addresses its primary limitation: the reliance on an implicit, feature-based representation of scene content. By introducing the ViT backbone and the SPADE mechanism, we have shown that making the model's understanding of scene structure more explicit leads to more accurate predictions of human attentional deployment.

\paragraph{Bridging the Gap with Classic Saliency and Object-Based Attention.} Our findings also resonate with a long-standing dialogue in vision science that dates back to the earliest saliency models. The classic, bottom-up models of Itti, Koch, and Niebur were based on low-level feature contrast \cite{itti1998}, a purely space-based view of attention. Subsequent research has increasingly pointed to the importance of "proto-objects" and object-level units in guiding attention \cite{10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011512}. Our work provides a modern, deep learning-based validation of this object-centric view. By demonstrating that an explicit segmentation signal—an operational definition of "objects"—improves prediction, we provide strong computational evidence that the parsing of a scene into coherent units is a fundamental component of attentional guidance, not merely an epiphenomenon of feature saliency.

\paragraph{A Framework for Testing Theories of Perception.} Perhaps the most significant implication of our work is methodological. The `DinoGaze-SPADE` architecture is more than just a high-performing model; it is a flexible scientific tool. It provides a concrete framework for quantitatively testing theories about the relationship between perception and action. As highlighted by the foundational work of Vacher et al. (2023), it is now possible to measure an individual's subjective perceptual segmentation of a scene \cite{vacher2023measuring}. Our model provides the means to directly inject these personalized perceptual maps and measure their impact on gaze prediction, opening the door to a new generation of individualized models of visual attention.

\section{Limitations and Future Directions}
\label{sec:discussion_future}

Despite its successes, our work has several limitations that point toward exciting avenues for future research.

\paragraph{The Nature of Unsupervised Segmentation.} Our current approach relies on generic, unsupervised segmentation masks. While effective, these masks are not a perfect proxy for human perception and may contain artifacts or boundaries that are not meaningful to a human observer. The promising results from our collaborators (Brusco et al., 2025) suggest that the greatest gains are yet to be realized. The next logical and most exciting step is to replace our generic masks with the \textbf{Perceptual Segmentation Maps (PSMs)} measured from individual human observers using the Vacher et al. paradigm. This will allow us to move from population-level to truly \textbf{personalized gaze prediction}, testing the powerful hypothesis that knowing \textit{how a specific person sees the world} allows us to better predict \textit{where that specific person will look}.

\paragraph{Beyond Static Images.} Our current models, like most in the field, operate on static images. Real-world vision is a dynamic process of interacting with a constantly changing environment. Extending the `DinoGaze-SPADE` architecture to video, where segmentation must be consistent across time (object tracking), is a non-trivial but crucial next step.

\paragraph{Exploring Deeper Architectural Integration.} While SPADE is a powerful mechanism, it primarily modulates the saliency network. The scanpath and fixation selection networks remain standard modules. Future work could explore more deeply integrated architectures, such as using cross-attention mechanisms to allow the scanpath history to directly query the semantically segmented feature maps, potentially enabling the model to learn even more complex, context-dependent viewing strategies.

In conclusion, this thesis has demonstrated the significant and quantifiable benefit of integrating explicit structural information into models of human gaze. By developing a new state-of-the-art architecture, we have not only advanced the predictive power of these models but have also provided a powerful new framework for the scientific investigation of the intricate link between visual perception and attention.
